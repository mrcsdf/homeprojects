Marcus — straight talk: the trim in your photo is a single-handle, pressure-balanced tub/shower valve (not a true thermostatic mixer). There’s no electronic “thermostat” in there. Temperature is controlled by a cartridge plus an anti-scald limit stop. When you only get lukewarm or the temp hunts, the cartridge’s balancing spool is usually stuck or the limit stop is set too cold.

Below is a tight, step-by-step to diagnose and fix it. Read once, then do it.

⸻

0) Tools & supplies
	•	1/8” or 3/32” hex key (set screw), Phillips screwdriver
	•	Utility knife, small flat screwdriver (prying caps/clips)
	•	Adjustable wrench or deep socket for the bonnet nut
	•	Needle-nose pliers (for the U-shaped retaining clip)
	•	Silicone plumber’s grease (NOT petroleum)
	•	White vinegar or CLR for descaling
	•	Cartridge (best fix): buy brand-specific once ID’d (Moen 1222/1225, Delta Monitor RP19804/RP46074, Pfister 974-042, Kohler Rite-Temp GP800820, etc.).
	•	Thermometer (any kitchen probe) & towel.

1) Quick checks (2 minutes)
	1.	Verify hot at the sink. Run the nearest lavatory hot only for 30–60 s. If it’s 120 °F-ish, your water heater is fine; the shower is the problem.
	2.	Symptom map
	•	Max temp is just warm → limit stop set too low or cartridge stuck.
	•	Temp swings when a toilet runs/washer starts → cartridge spool sticky.
	•	Handle rotates but temp barely changes → cartridge bad.

2) Adjust the anti-scald limit stop (often all you need)
	1.	Kill water only if you must. For stop adjustment you can usually leave water on.
	2.	Remove handle. There’s either a little cap to pop off OR a set screw under the lever. Back out the screw, pull the handle off.
	3.	Expose the stop. Remove the decorative dome/sleeve by hand. You’ll see a plastic dial/stop ring.
	4.	Increase travel toward HOT. Rotate the stop one or two notches in the direction that allows the handle to turn further into the red side. (Brands differ: if you guess wrong, put it back and rotate the other way.)
	5.	Test. Refit the handle loosely, turn on, let run 60 s, measure water at the tub spout. Target 115–120 °F max. Don’t exceed 120 °F for scald safety.
	6.	If that fixed it, reassemble snug, you’re done. If not, proceed.

3) Service or replace the cartridge (the real fix)

Plan on replacing; cleaning sometimes revives it but isn’t guaranteed.

	1.	Shut off water. Use the integral stop screws on the valve if present; otherwise close the house main and open the tub to relieve pressure.
	2.	Strip the trim. Handle off (as above), remove the sleeve and escutcheon (two screws). You’ll see the cartridge body with either a bonnet nut or a U-clip.
	3.	Lock orientation. Mark the top of the cartridge and valve body with a Sharpie. (Prevents reversed hot/cold on reassembly.)
	4.	Remove retainer.
	•	U-clip: pull it straight up with needle-nose.
	•	Bonnet nut: loosen with a wrench; don’t twist the valve body in the wall.
	5.	Extract the cartridge. Wiggle straight out. If it’s stubborn, reinstall the handle to use as a puller or use a cartridge puller. Don’t pry against the valve body.
	6.	Decision: clean or replace
	•	Clean (temporary): Soak the cartridge 30–60 min in warm vinegar/CLR. Work the stem back and forth until the balancing spool moves freely. Rinse well.
	•	Replace (preferred): Match by brand/shape. Common ones:
	•	Moen Posi-Temp: 1222 (short, fat cylinder, U-clip)
	•	Moen Moentrol/old 1-handle: 1225 (thinner)
	•	Delta Monitor 1300/1400: RP19804 (brass) or RP46074 (plastic)
	•	Pfister 0X8/old: 974-042
	•	Kohler Rite-Temp: GP800820/GP1016515
	7.	Prep & install. Lightly grease O-rings with silicone. Insert the cartridge in the same orientation as marked (hot on left). Seat fully. Reinstall clip or bonnet; snug only—do not overtighten.
	8.	Flush. Before the trim goes back on: with the stem installed, crack water on briefly and run the valve mid-position to blast debris out the spout (aim a towel).
	9.	Reassemble trim and re-set the limit stop (Step 2) to cap max temp around 120 °F.
	10.	Final test. Run full cold→full hot. Temperature should rise smoothly with no surging.

4) If it’s still wrong
	•	Reversed hot/cold? Shut off, pull cartridge, rotate it 180°, reinstall.
	•	No hot anywhere? Water heater or a closed valve upstream, not the shower.
	•	Still surging: The valve’s internal check valves may be fouled (some cartridges include them; others are behind small caps in the body). Clean/replace, or replace the whole valve if parts are obsolete.
	•	Drips after shutoff: Cartridge seals nicked—replace cartridge.

5) Identify your brand fast (so you can buy the right cartridge)
	•	Look for a logo on the escutcheon (Delta, Moen, Pfister, Kohler).
	•	Retaining style: U-clip screams Moen, Delta, Pfister, Kohler Rite-Temp. Bonnet nut is common on Delta.
	•	Take the old cartridge to the store; match by eye and part number. Worst case, post a straight-on photo of the cartridge and I’ll ID it.

6) Forward-looking upgrade (optional)

If this valve is older and you care about precise temperature, swap to a thermostatic valve (true temperature control + separate volume). It costs more but is rock-solid and easier to calibrate.
